## What the editing does (own words)
- It is CUT, not a moving long take. Camera angle, distance and subject change on almost every beat.
- Alternates: (a) wide / establishing with the whole field and dogs small in the distance, (b) low tracking shots at ground
  level beside a running animal, (c) medium two-shots of the allies in low angle, (d) EXTREME close-ups (an eye, an ear, a
  jaw, the wound, a patch of skin), (e) from-below shots looking up into a roaring mouth, (f) reaction shots of the watching
  dog, (g) shots of the monster rearing up against the sky.
- Impact beats are built from 3-5 very short shots: wind-up detail -> contact detail -> reaction face -> wide aftermath.
- Rhythm: fast burst (several cuts in one second) -> a held close-up (2-3 s) for feeling -> burst again.
- Backgrounds: outdoors, a green meadow, big dramatic clouds, blue mountains, rock piles; hard slash/flash effects on impact.
- Sound carries the cuts (roars, impact, shouts); there is almost no spoken text during the action.

## Rules for our scene (shot-list first)
- Build the scene from a numbered SHOT LIST with hard cuts: each shot has a camera position, lens (fov), target, duration
  (0.1-3 s) and what moves. Provide at least: wide, ground-level tracking, extreme close-up (eye / jaw / wound), from-below
  roar, low-angle two-shot, reaction close-up, rearing silhouette against the sky, whip pan, short flash cuts at impact.
- Never use the same framing twice in a row. Mix lens widths (very wide fov 70-90 for ground-level; tight 15-25 for extreme
  close-ups). Dutch tilt for tension. Occasional shake and speed lines at impact.
- Dialogue in the action: keep it short; let cuts and sound carry the beat. Subtitles only for spoken lines.
