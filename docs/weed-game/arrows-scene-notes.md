# Four Arrows scene (P4 / Kaibutsu fight): research notes

Gathered from search results (fan wikis and Wikipedia snippets; Fandom and silverfang pages block direct fetching, so
only the search summaries were available). Treat as [UNCERTAIN] where sources disagree. Own words.

## Beats (anime version, most sources)
- Jerome's last four subordinates are North, Robert, Rocker and Heuler (he originally had 13).
- Arrow 1, Robert: charges first and alone; P4 bites his head and kills him. In the anime he fails to do real damage
  but "completes his mission" and dies with honor (manga: he dies instantly without the spoken last words).
- Arrow 2, Rocker: follows Robert, sinks his teeth into P4's chest, then dies.
- Arrow 3, Heuler: follows Rocker and bites into the chest wound the others opened; P4 forcibly tears him off and he dies
  (manga variant: Heuler dies tearing at the weak point to widen the wound for Jerome; another entry says his head is
  crushed).
- Purpose: each dog bites into the same weak point on the chest so the wound deepens each time, trying to reach the heart
  (the "arrows" shot at the same spot). Jerome is the last/"fourth" arrow in the Japanese Wikipedia wording.
- [UNCERTAIN] Japanese Wikipedia says four arrows; some fan wikis say three arrows, and the order of Robert and Rocker
  is not consistent between sources.
- The fight ends when Weed gets the monster impaled on a branch (driftwood) in the river and it dies (heart pierced).

## User's description of the reference clip (the real anime scene)
- Strong speed lines as each dog charges, one after another. Each bite goes deeper into the skin than the previous one.
- P4 is normally four-legged; when one of the arrows hits, the impact makes it rear up onto its hind legs for a moment.
  In one hit it falls over onto its back.
- Heavy voice acting and shouting; real flesh sounds (teeth sinking in).
- Camera: outdoors on a green meadow, dramatic clouded sky, blue mountains; extreme close-ups of eyes, teeth and the
  wound; fast cuts to wides; low angles; hard slash effect on impact; quiet reaction close-ups.
